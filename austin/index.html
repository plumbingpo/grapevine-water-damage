Simple Ways to Keep an Austin Home Dry

Houses tell you when water is getting where it should not. The signs are easy to miss if you do not know them, so here is what our technicians look for in Austin homes. Blistering paint, ceiling stains, warped baseboards and floorboards that curl at the edges each point to water behind or beneath them. Add a stale odor in an enclosed space, a loose toilet, or a faucet that keeps dripping.

Taken singly they are minor, though every one of them gets more expensive with time. Renew the caulk around baths and showers once it splits, because water that slips behind tile stays hidden for a long time. Test a sump pump several times a year by filling the pit until the float trips and the motor runs. Keep stored items off the basement or garage floor on shelves or in plastic bins.

Good upkeep lowers the odds, but it cannot remove them. Fewer and smaller leaks are still a real gain, especially compared with an Austin home full of drying machines for most of a week.

Where Water Damage Starts in Austin Properties

Water usually escapes at the same few vulnerable spots in a home. The list below covers what we are asked to clean up most for Austin owners.

Water heaters: Over the years, mineral deposits settle in a tank and corrode the metal underneath. The first sign is often a rusty puddle or a damp ring on the floor around the base. If the tank ruptures, it keeps refilling and draining onto the floor until someone closes the cold supply valve. Heaters in a hallway closet or attic can ruin flooring and ceilings well beyond the utility area.

Roofs: One strong storm is enough to peel back shingles and push water beneath the roof covering. Leaks also start around chimneys, vent pipes and skylights where the sealant has dried out. Water travels along rafters before it drips, so the wet spot inside may be far from the hole outside. On Austin roof leak calls, our crew removes saturated insulation, sets drying equipment and can cover the opening until a roofer arrives.

Freezing: Depending on the season, a hard overnight freeze can burst lines that were fine the day before. Outdoor spigots, sprinkler feeds and pipes in crawl spaces or along outside walls are hit first. A house left empty or a wing kept unheated faces the greatest risk. If a faucet only trickles on a freezing morning, shut off the water and call before the pipe thaws.

Backups: A drain that gurgles or several fixtures that slow down together point to a blockage in the main line. Once it closes off fully, anything you flush or drain returns at the lowest opening in the house. Since the waste is hazardous, we extract it and then sanitize concrete, tile and other hard surfaces. Stay out of the affected rooms and avoid running water until the line is cleared.

Seepage: A wet line along the bottom of a foundation wall is a sign that water is building up in the soil next to it. Short downspouts, soil that slopes toward the house and blocked footing drains are frequent causes. Early clues include powdery white residue on the concrete, flaking wall paint and air that always smells musty. We remove any standing water in Austin homes, dry the walls and slab, and explain what likely caused it.
